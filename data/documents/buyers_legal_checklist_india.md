# Buyer's Legal Checklist for Property Purchase in India (Sample)

Disclaimer: This checklist is a generic, illustrative summary for demonstration purposes only. It is not legal advice. Laws and rates vary by state and change over time; consult a qualified property lawyer and your bank/lender before relying on any of this.

## 1. Title Verification
- Obtain and review the chain of title documents (parent documents) for at least the last 30 years, if available.
- Check the Encumbrance Certificate (EC) from the Sub-Registrar's office to confirm there are no pending mortgages, liens or litigation.
- Verify the seller's identity matches the title documents (PAN, Aadhaar, or passport).
- For inherited property, confirm succession/legal-heir documents and, where applicable, a probated will.

## 2. Approvals and Compliance
- Confirm sanctioned building plan approval from the local municipal authority.
- Verify Occupancy Certificate (OC) and Completion Certificate (CC) for ready-to-move properties.
- For under-construction projects, confirm RERA (Real Estate Regulatory Authority) registration number and check the project's status on the state RERA portal.
- Check property tax receipts to confirm dues are cleared and the property is not under any government acquisition notice.

## 3. Stamp Duty and Registration
- Stamp duty rates vary by state (commonly in the range of roughly 3-7% of property value, subject to change and local exemptions such as those for women buyers in some states).
- Registration charges are typically a smaller additional percentage, again state-dependent.
- Ensure the Sale Deed is registered at the Sub-Registrar's office with both parties present (or through valid power of attorney).

## 4. Home Loan Documentation (if financing)
Lenders typically require:
- Identity and address proof (PAN, Aadhaar, passport)
- Income proof: salary slips / IT returns / Form 16 for salaried buyers, or audited financials for self-employed buyers
- Bank statements (typically 6-12 months)
- Property documents: sale agreement, title documents, approved plan, EC, OC/CC where applicable
- The lender will typically conduct its own legal and technical due diligence before disbursing the loan.

## 5. Society / Association Compliance
- Confirm No Objection Certificate (NOC) from the housing society or builder, where applicable, for transfer of ownership.
- Review society bye-laws, maintenance charges, and any pending dues (maintenance, parking, utility) associated with the unit.

## 6. Other Practical Checks
- Physically inspect the property and compare carpet area / built-up area against what is stated in documents.
- Check for any pending litigation by searching the relevant court records where feasible.
- Consider title insurance where available, and always retain certified copies of all documents.

This checklist is intentionally general. State-specific rules (e.g., Maharashtra, Karnataka, Telangana, Tamil Nadu, Gujarat, Delhi NCR) differ in stamp duty rates, registration procedure and RERA implementation details -- confirm current requirements with a local lawyer before proceeding.
